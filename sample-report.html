What is inside the report.

The full sample is a real assessment of a real house, published with the client's identity changed. This page walks through its structure so you can judge the product before paying for it.

The sample at a glance

One house, fully assessed.

143checkpoints worked through across 12 categories
112findings recorded in this particular home
41of those findings rated high priority

Ten of the 112 findings cost nothing at all to resolve. That ratio is typical rather than remarkable: a meaningful share of what makes a home unsafe is a rug, a cord, a stored item or an unlocked cupboard, and costs only the decision to deal with it.

The sample is a genuine assessment of a property belonging to Novo Industries Inc., published under a changed name and address. The findings and measurements are real; the identity is not. Contractor names appearing in it are placeholders, clearly labelled as such in the document.

Structure

How the report is laid out.

Front

One-page summary

The whole assessment compressed to a page: how many findings, how many are urgent, what the first phase costs, and what to do this week. Written for the family member who will not read forty pages.

Method

Scope and method

What was assessed, what was not, what equipment was used, and the limits of the exercise. Stated at the front rather than buried at the back.

Core

Findings by category

Twelve sections, each working through its checkpoints. Every finding gets a priority, what was observed, the measurement where one applies, the recommended fix, a cost range, and a photograph where the finding is high priority.

Core

Next Dollar Impact ranking

Every finding in the home, re-sorted into a single list by safety impact per dollar. This is the section most families use first.

Plan

Two-year phased plan

Three phases — this month, within three months, within six to twelve months — with a cost total for each. Designed so a family can stop after phase one and still have removed most of the risk.

Plan

Funding notes

Programs that may apply, noted against the findings they relate to, with links to the official pages. Not an application and not a promise of eligibility.

Action

Hiring the work out

Three screened local contractors, one per primary trade, plus what to ask any contractor for before work starts — a $2M certificate of insurance and a WCB clearance letter.

Back

Measurements and standards

The dimensional record, and the CSA/ASC B652:23 clause references that some findings draw on, kept as a quiet reference line so the finding itself stays in plain language.

A finding, in full

Every finding follows the same shape.

So the report can be read straight through or dipped into. Priority, what was observed, the measurement where one applies, why it matters, the recommended fix, a cost range, and a photograph where the finding is high priority.

Photographs are deliberately not attached to every finding. A family should not receive an album of things in their home that are perfectly fine.

A finding, in full

What one line of the report looks like.

Every finding follows the same shape, so the report can be read straight through or dipped into. The elements are:

PriorityHigh, medium or low, assigned by the ranking method rather than by feel
What was observedPlain description, written for a family rather than a trade
MeasurementWhere the finding has a dimension. Findings such as “no grab bar present” have none, and the report does not invent one
Why it mattersThe mechanism — what would have to happen for this to cause an injury
Recommended fixSpecific enough for a contractor to quote from without a second visit
Cost rangeA planning figure drawn from typical local pricing. Not a quote
PhotographIncluded for high-priority findings. Deliberately not for every finding — a family should not receive an album of things in their home that are fine
Standard referenceWhere one applies, a quiet CSA/ASC B652:23 clause line under the finding

Limits

What the report will not tell you.

Cost figures are planning estimates built from typical local pricing, not quotes. Real quotes vary with access, materials and who is available. The report is prepared for you alone — a buyer, lender or contractor may not rely on it. It carries no opinion on the condition, structure, systems, value or insurability of the home, and it is not a medical, clinical or occupational therapy assessment of any person.

If something in a delivered report is factually wrong or incomplete, tell us within ten business days and it is corrected at no charge. Full terms: Terms of Service.
