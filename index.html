Straight Answers

The three things families ask before booking.

"Isn't this free through the hospital?"

An occupational therapist assessment through the health system is a real and valuable service, and where one is offered, take it. It is also typically tied to a specific discharge or funding request, focused on the immediate clinical need, subject to wait times, and does not produce costed recommendations, a phased budget, or vetted contractors. This is the document you hand a builder. The two work well together — several clients have had both.

"Will my mother be upset by this?"

It is the most common worry, and reasonably so — nobody wants to feel assessed in their own home. The visit is run as a conversation with the person who lives there, not an inspection of them. The framing that works is the honest one: this is about keeping the house working so they can stay in it. Most visits end with the homeowner pointing out things they had been quietly working around for years.

“We were worried the assessment would feel like an inspection. It didn’t.”David & Susan R., Cochrane

"We're not ready to renovate."

Then the report is more useful, not less. A large share of findings in a typical home cost under $250 — a rail, a light, a threshold, a bar in the right spot at the right height. The two-year plan exists precisely so nothing has to happen at once. Knowing the full picture is what stops money going to the wrong thing first.

Funding

Money that is already available.

Four programs commonly apply to the modifications this report recommends. The report identifies which findings qualify for which program, so the applications are straightforward.

SHARP

Up to $40,000

Alberta. Low-interest home adaptation loan at 4.45% for homeowners 65+ with household income of $75,000 or less and at least 25% equity. Covers bathroom adaptations, stair lifts, doorway widening and more. A separate grant exists for those who do not qualify for the loan.

Program details at Alberta.ca →

AADL

Equipment funding

Alberta. Alberta Aids to Daily Living cost-shares essential equipment and supplies for people with long-term needs — including many mobility and bathroom safety items identified in the report.

Program details at Alberta.ca →

Home Accessibility Tax Credit

Up to $3,000 back

Federal. A 15% non-refundable credit on up to $20,000 of eligible renovation expenses per year, for those 65+ or eligible for the disability tax credit. Claimable by a qualifying senior or, in many cases, a supporting family member.

Program details at Canada.ca →

Multigenerational Renovation Credit

Up to $7,500 back

Federal. A 15% refundable credit on up to $50,000 of costs to build a secondary suite for a senior or an adult with a disability — relevant when the plan is a parent moving in rather than staying put.

Measurement

"Looks narrow" is not a specification.

A tape measure is good enough for a renovation estimate. It is not good enough when you need to know whether a walker clears a doorframe, a bed is at the right height for a safe transfer, or a threshold lip is a trip hazard.

What most reports say

"The hallway looks narrow."

No contractor can quote from this. No family can plan from it. Nothing here tells you whether the problem is real, urgent, or expensive.

What this report says

"The clearance between the dresser and the bed measures 62 cm — a walker requires 75 cm."

A number, a standard, and a gap. Now the problem is provable, the fix is obvious, and the trade can price it without a second visit.

Every specific finding is backed by a number — doorway widths, clearance measurements, threshold heights, fixture dimensions — captured on site with a professional laser distance measurer accurate to ±1.5 mm.

How The Costs Are Built

Not an estimate. The average of three real quotes.

Most reports guess at cost, or quote a range wide enough to be useless. Every price in this report is the averaged rate of three vetted contractors in that trade, working from their current price sheets. The number you read is what the work actually costs in Calgary right now.

1

Three vetted contractors per trade

Plumbing, electrical, carpentry, flooring, stair and lift installation — each trade is covered by three independently vetted local contractors, not one.

2

Current price sheets on file

Each holds a rate card with us for the modifications this assessment recommends — grab bar installs, rail runs, threshold ramps, lighting circuits, lever hardware.

3

Your report shows the average

Each finding carries the blended rate of all three for that specific work, so the number in the report is a fair market price — not the cheapest, not the dearest, not a guess.

What this means for your budget

  • Three contractors per trade
    Real choice on every line of work, and a genuine second and third opinion built in.
  • Priced from live rate cards
    Refreshed on a set schedule, so the report reflects this year's labour and material costs.
  • No referral fees, no commissions
    Nothing changes hands between the trades and this business, in either direction.
  • 5-point vetting standard
    Licensing, insurance, references, accessibility experience, and responsiveness — all three names checked the same way.

Why the average, and not the lowest?

The cheapest quote is the one most likely to move once the work starts. Averaging three vetted contractors gives a figure a family can plan against and take to a funding application without it collapsing at the first site visit.

You are free to use any of the three, or a trade of your own — the report is yours and the pricing travels with it.

Whichever you choose, the price in the report is the starting point for that conversation, not the final word on it.

Every figure is the averaged rate of three vetted contractors, current at the date the report is issued. It is there so you can plan and budget with confidence, and so you can tell straight away whether a quote you receive sits in the normal range.
