Home

When you’re worried about Mom or Dad at home.

Most calls come from adult children. A parent has fallen, is coming home from hospital, or is starting to forget things, and you need to know what to change first without being sold anything.

What usually prompts the call.

Coming home from hospital

A clear list of what must be done before discharge day, in order, with prices.

After a fall or a near miss

Every trip, reach and transfer point measured, so you fix the cause and not just the room it happened in.

Memory is changing

A dedicated set of checks for Alzheimer’s and other dementias: exits, the stove, medications and wayfinding.

You live out of town

The report arrives as a PDF by email, so you and your brothers and sisters can read the same plan from wherever you are.

Talking to your parent about it.

Many parents hear “safety assessment” as “you can’t cope.” It helps to be clear about what this is. The report describes the home, not the person. No one tests your parent, and the report never offers a view on whether they should stay in their home.

It also helps that nobody arrives to sell anything. Your parent can hear the findings, keep the report, and decide what happens and when. A family member or your parent shows us how the home is really used, and that is the whole visit.

One plan the whole family can agree on.

Families often disagree about what is needed and what it should cost. A ranked list with a Calgary price beside every fix turns that argument into a plan: what to do this month, within 3 months, and in 6 to 12 months, and which funding can help pay.

Nothing to sell you after the visit.
