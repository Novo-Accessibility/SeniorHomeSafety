Doors, handles and thresholds.

A round knob is easy to ignore until hands get stiff. A doorway is wide enough until a walker has to fit through it. These details are measured, not guessed.

What we check at doors.

Every finding is photographed and, where it has a dimension, laser-measured against CSA B652 reference points.

  • Round knobs that are hard to grip and turn, especially with arthritis or wet hands.
  • The clear width of each doorway, measured, for a walker or wheelchair now or later.
  • Raised thresholds and sills that become a trip or a barrier. The reference calls for 13 mm or less.
  • Heavy doors and strong door closers.
  • Locks and deadbolts that are hard to turn, and whether someone could get in quickly in an emergency.
  • Whether the front door can be answered safely: a viewer or window at a usable height.

Fixes the report often recommends.

These are examples, not a package. The report recommends only what this home needs, and says when something can wait.

Lever handles

Replace round knobs so a door opens with a push of the hand.

Offset hinges

Can widen a doorway without moving the frame.

Threshold ramps

Take the step out of a raised sill.

Adjusting door closers

Often a no-cost fix.

How each fix is priced.

Every fix in the report gets its own Calgary price from our cost index: the typical time the job takes, published trade rates, and current prices for mid-range parts. It is a planning figure, not a quote, so you know what a fair quote looks like before you call anyone.

You choose who does the work: any contractor, a handy family member, or nobody yet. The report ranks the fixes so the highest-value ones come first.
