How contractors are screened.

Every report names three local contractors, one per primary trade. No money moves between us and any of them, in either direction — and the checks behind the names are the ones anyone can verify on public record.

No referral fees. No commissions. Ever.

No commission, referral fee, kickback or other benefit is accepted from any contractor or supplier named in a report, and none is paid to them. This is a term of the client agreement, not a policy that can quietly change. A ranked list of what to fix first is worth nothing if the ranking follows the money.

The checks

What is verified before a contractor is named.

Each of these is checked on public record rather than taken on a contractor's word, because a check you perform yourself is worth more than an answer you were given.

Alberta corporate registration

That the business exists, is active, and is registered in Alberta under the name it trades under. Checked against the Alberta Corporate Registry.

WCB clearance

Workers' Compensation Board account in good standing, confirmed through WCB Alberta's own clearance system. If a worker is injured on your property and the contractor has no WCB coverage, the exposure can land on you.

Prepaid contracting licence

Checked through Service Alberta's licence search where the work involves payment before completion. A missing licence where one is required is a meaningful signal.

A personal introduction call

An actual conversation. Whether they return calls, whether they understand accessibility work as distinct from general renovation, and whether they will talk plainly to an older client.

Insurance

Why insurance verification stays with you.

Contractor insurance is the one item deliberately not verified on your behalf, and the reason matters.

You hire the contractor. The contract is between you and them, and the exposure if something goes wrong is yours, not ours. A certificate of insurance confirmed to a third party weeks before your job starts proves very little: policies lapse, coverage changes, and a certificate naming us is not a certificate naming you.

So the report shows you how to ask. When you call any contractor for a quote, ask for a $2,000,000 certificate of general liability insurance and a current WCB clearance letter, issued in your name, before work begins. The report gives you the wording. It takes one sentence on the phone, and it is worth more than any assurance we could pass along second-hand.

This applies to any contractor, not just ours.

If you find your own tradesperson — which plenty of families do — the same two requests apply. Nothing about this depends on using a name from the report.

Matching

How the three names are chosen.

Contractors are matched to the work the report recommends: one per primary trade, rather than three general renovators or three of whoever was available. A report calling for grab bars, a stair rail and improved lighting produces different names from one calling for a bathroom reconfiguration.

Three, not one, because a single name is a referral and three is a starting point. Comparing quotes is the family's job, and it is a job worth doing — the spread on the same scope of work is routinely wider than people expect.

The choice is entirely yours. Nothing in the report requires you to use any of the three, and the cost figures in the report are planning estimates rather than quotes from these contractors.
